# Slide 6: What we learned

Act: Discovery. Audiences: Leadership, Staff.

## The question it answers

What did we learn about customers this period, and how sure are we?

## Required data

Interview notes, prototype and experiment results, eval error analysis.

| Source | What the slide reads | Required |
|---|---|---|
| Research and call notes | Interview notes, prototype and experiment results | Yes |
| Evals and traces | Eval error analysis | No |

## Minimum evidence to render

At least one of these blocks, filled from computed data: `table`, `tiles`.
If the data is missing, put a `data_needed` block in its place with an owner and a due date. Never fill the gap with prose.

## The rule

Evidence comes from real customers. AI can summarise interviews and it can't stand in for them. Name the risk each test addressed (value, usability, feasibility or viability). Show at least one thing you killed.

## Time window

State the window on the slide's `asof` line. In the example: "Sources: research repository, experiment log, eval suite v14. Q3 FY26."

## Titles

- Good: "Two of five opportunities are validated. We killed one after a prototype test." (a claim someone can check).
- Bad: "What we learned" (a label; it claims nothing).

## Reading

- Teresa Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits (2021). In 2026 she said she would add a chapter on evals as a discovery habit, and that cheap building is no reason to build everything ([airfocus, Jun 2026](https://airfocus.com/blog/how-ai-is-changing-product-management/)).
- [Marty Cagan, The AI Productivity Paradox](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/) (Jul 2026). Build to learn in discovery, build to earn in delivery. His four risks are value, usability, feasibility and viability.
- [Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar on error analysis](https://hamel.dev/notes/llm/ai-product-engineering/evals-error-analysis.html) (Jun 2026). A product manager or domain expert should read the failures.
- [First Round, The Art of Evals at Figma](https://review.firstround.com/figma-ai-eval-process/) (May 2025). People at the centre of AI product evaluation.
