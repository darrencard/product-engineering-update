# Slide 1: The answer

Act: Open. Audiences: Leadership, Board, Staff.

## The question it answers

Are we on track, what changed, and what do you need from me?

## Required data

Nothing new. This slide is a rollup of slides 5 to 18, written last.

Nothing new. It rolls up the other slides in its cut.

## Minimum evidence to render

At least one of these blocks, filled from computed data: `cards`, `table`.
If the data is missing, put a `data_needed` block in its place with an owner and a due date. Never fill the gap with prose.

## The rule

One headline sentence. At most 3 wins and 2 headwinds. State what changed since the last update. Every ask on this slide appears again on slide 18.

## Time window

State the window on the slide's `asof` line. In the example: "Data as of 30 Sep 2026. Status words: landed, watch, stalled."

## Titles

- Good: "Retention is on plan, expansion is behind, and we need one decision on model spend." (a claim someone can check).
- Bad: "The answer" (a label; it claims nothing).

## Reading

- [Engineering Management, Pyramid Communication: An Actionable Guide](https://engmanage.substack.com/p/pyramid-communication) (Nov 2025). Lead with the recommendation, then the supporting arguments, then the data.
- [Cedric Chin, The Amazon Weekly Business Review](https://commoncog.com/the-amazon-weekly-business-review/) (Jul 2024). A weekly metrics review works by exposing cause and effect, and depends on leadership discipline more than tooling.
- [Amanda Herson, Founder Collective, Debates Over Updates](https://foundercollective.com/?p=14226) (Mar 2026). Start with what changed since the last meeting, send data ahead, and build the meeting around 1 decision.
- [Product School, Product Leadership](https://productschool.com/blog/leadership/product-leadership) (Nov 2026). One story cut three ways, why this problem for teams, why this bet for executives, why now for the board.
