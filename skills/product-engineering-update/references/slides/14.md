# Slide 14: Risks & assumptions

Act: Next investments. Audiences: Leadership, Board.

## The question it answers

What could break the plan, and what are we doing about it?

## Required data

A risk register with likelihood, impact, owner and date. A short list of assumptions the plan rests on.

| Source | What the slide reads | Required |
|---|---|---|
| Documents | A risk register with likelihood, impact, owner and date | Yes |

## Minimum evidence to render

At least one of these blocks, filled from computed data: `table`.
If the data is missing, put a `data_needed` block in its place with an owner and a due date. Never fill the gap with prose.

## The rule

5 risks at most. Each mitigation names a person. Include model, vendor, eval and review risks alongside the usual ones.

## Time window

State the window on the slide's `asof` line. In the example: "Source: risk register, reviewed 2 Oct 2026."

## Titles

- Good: "Our largest risk is inference cost tied to one provider. Routing is the fix." (a claim someone can check).
- Bad: "Risks & assumptions" (a label; it claims nothing).

## Reading

- McKinsey, A new operating model for a new world (Jun 2025). 12 elements and 4 outcomes, clarity, speed, skills and commitment. It reports a 30% gap between strategy potential and delivery, even at strong companies.
- [Cagan, Build vs Buy in the Age of AI](https://www.svpg.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox/) and related SVPG pieces (2025 to 2026).
- [Robert Kimani, LeadDev, Your engineering strategy has a new failure mode](https://leaddev.com/leadership/your-engineering-strategy-has-a-new-failure-mode) (Oct 2026). Match how reversible a commitment is to how strong its evidence is. Platform migrations can take years to reverse.
