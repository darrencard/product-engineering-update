Q3 2026: the agent is working. Activation, change failure rate and inference cost are behind.

Period: 1 Jul – 30 Sep 2026 · Data as of 30 Sep 2026 · Author: the CPTO · Prepared 25 Nov 2026

Summary

  • North star beat target. Loads cleared with no manual touch averaged 23.1K/week, against a 22K target. That's up 26% on Q2 (18.4K).
  • Activation is the biggest gap. It's 41% against a 50% target, up from 39% in Q2. Guided activation shipped, but the files don't show any lift from it yet. Self-serve carrier onboarding, the other activation commitment, didn't ship.
  • Agent quality is better, but not at the bar yet. The v14 evals passed 86% of cases (v13: 82%), and cost per task fell 20% to $0.33. The strategy target is 90%.
  • Inference spend was 28% over plan ($612K against $480K) while revenue beat plan by 3% ($10.3M against $10.0M). Most inference spend runs through one model provider, which is the top risk on the register.
  • Engineering ships more often but breaks more. Deploys rose to 4.0/week (from 3.0). Change failure rate rose to 7.7% (range: under 5%) and PR review time to 13h (range: under 10h), despite the release checklist shipping.
  • Churn outweighed new business in the billing export. Four churned accounts took $1.11M of ARR, more than the $0.90M from six new accounts. Expansion in 15 accounts carried net growth (+5.1%). Net revenue retention was 96%.
  • Decision: the per-load pricing pilot. Its deadline was 4 Nov 2026, which has passed as of this draft. The files don't record an outcome (see Decision).

Evidence relevant to the decision

  • Price lost deals. 6 of 17 lost deals in the CRM export cite price. The other 11 cite "No TMS integration".
  • Cost per load. Q3 inference spend divided by loads cleared comes to about $2.02 per cleared load ($612K ÷ roughly 304K loads, i.e. 23.1K/week × 13.1 weeks). This overstates the true per-load cost because inference also serves loads that still needed a manual touch. It's still a useful ceiling when setting a per-load price.
  • Strategy fit. The strategy already commits to pricing per load ("automating the exception, priced per load"), so the pilot tests our stated policy rather than a new direction.

Data gaps and caveats

Owner for closing these gaps: the VP Engineering, due Q4.

  1. ARR doesn't reconcile. The billing export totals $10.91M ARR across 42 accounts at September. The finance close shows Q3 revenue of $10.3M, roughly $41M annualised. Progress against the $50M objective can't be stated until the two agree.
  2. Lead time. Deploys aren't linked to PRs, so commit-to-production lead time isn't measurable. This update uses PR open-to-merge time as a proxy.
  3. Suspiciously uniform source-control data. Every Q2 PR shows 52h to merge and 9h of review, and every Q3 PR shows 38h and 13h. The incident restore times are uniform in the same way. Confirm whether this is an aggregated export rather than per-item data.
  4. Commitments and bets aren't traceable in the tracker. Tracker items have generic titles, so shipped status and dates come from the intake only. Without ship dates, neither bet's stop rule can be checked.
  5. No readings after launch. There's no eval after Multi-stop reroutes shipped, and no result from the Guided activation live test.
  6. Undated exports. The CRM and support exports have no dates, so they may include deals and tickets from outside Q3.
  7. Request linkage. KES-388 (Carrier rating export) isn't linked to any tracker item. KES-412 is linked, but it isn't in the tracker export.
  8. Metric definitions. The eval harness reports "cost per task", while the commitment metric is "cost per successful task". Audit log export has no metric attached.
