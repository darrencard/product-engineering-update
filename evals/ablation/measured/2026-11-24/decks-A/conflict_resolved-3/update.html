Kestrel Labs · Product & Engineering update for leadership

Q3 2026 (1 Jul – 30 Sep 2026)

From the CPTO · Data as of 30 Sep 2026 · Prepared 24 Nov 2026 · Board version: board.html

Summary

  • The north star beat its target, but two systems give different numbers. PostHog, our source of truth, shows 23,100 loads cleared per week with no manual touch against a 22K target, up 25.5% on Q2. The warehouse shows 21,400, which is below target. We report PostHog. Until the two are reconciled, the “target met” call isn't settled.
  • ARR grew 5.1% to $10.91M in billing, but churn took back most of the new business. We added $900K from 6 new accounts and $741K from expansion, then lost $1.11M when 4 accounts churned. Net revenue retention was 96.4%. Finance close reports ARR of $10.1M, $811K below billing, and that gap is not yet explained.
  • Activation is the weakest input. It reached 41% against a 50% target, up only 2 points even after Guided activation shipped. Self-serve carrier onboarding was the one commitment we missed, and it targets this same metric.
  • We shipped faster, but quality slipped. Deploys rose to 4 per week and lead time fell to 38h. Change failure rate rose to 7.7% (target under 5%) and PR review time rose to 13h (target under 10h).
  • The work mix drifted toward keeping the lights on: 22% of completed work against a 15% plan. New capabilities came in at 31% against a 35% plan.
  • Integration is the gap that comes up most often in sales and support. It caused 11 of 17 lost deals and 30 of 55 support tickets. None of our strategy actions addresses it.

Decision needed

Approve a per-load pricing pilot.

Options: pilot with 10 customers, or wait a quarter.
Recommendation: run the pilot with 10 customers.
Why: Our strategy says we win mid-market brokers by pricing per load. We can't test that pricing without a pilot. Price was also the reason for 6 of the 17 deals we lost.
Deadline: 4 Nov 2026.

The deadline has passed. This update covers the quarter to 30 Sep but was prepared on 24 Nov. The input files don't record whether the pilot was approved. If it hasn't been decided, the decision is now overdue.

Churn is the issue to watch. We lost 10.7% of opening ARR in a single quarter. All four churned accounts filed an onboarding support ticket (T37–T40). That's a small sample and doesn't prove cause, but it matches the activation shortfall and points to onboarding.

ARR doesn't match across systems. Billing, the source of truth for ARR, shows $10.91M. Finance close reports $10.1M actual against an $11.0M plan, which is $811K lower. We report the billing figure. Finance close also lists Q3 “Revenue” as $10.3M (plan $10.0M). That's close to annual ARR, so the line is probably not quarterly revenue, and we don't calculate any ratios from it.
