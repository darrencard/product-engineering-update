Product & Engineering Update: 2026-Q3

Kestrel Labs · From the CPTO · Period 1 Jul – 30 Sep 2026 · Data as of 30 Sep 2026

Summary
  • The north star beat target. Loads cleared with no manual touch averaged 23,100 a week, against a target of 22K. That is up 26% from 18,400 in Q2.
  • The product works, but revenue growth is too slow for the objective. ARR in billing went from $10.38M to $10.91M (+5.1%). Net revenue retention was 96.4%: four accounts churned ($1.11M) and 21 of 40 did not expand. At this pace we will not get close to $50M.
  • Activation is the weak input. It reached 41% against a 50% target. All four churned accounts had filed onboarding tickets, and self-serve carrier onboarding was the one commitment we did not ship.
  • Two delivery measures are out of range. Change failure rate is 7.7% (target under 5%) and median PR review time is 13h (target under 10h). Both got worse than Q2.
  • Inference spend was $612K against a $480K plan (+28%). Cost per agent task fell 20%, but volume grew faster. Most of that spend sits with one provider.
  • One decision is overdue. The per-load pricing pilot had a 4 Nov 2026 deadline, which has passed. See below.

1. Decision needed

Approve a per-load pricing pilot. Options: pilot with 10 customers, or wait a quarter.
Recommendation: run the pilot with 10 customers.
The deadline was 4 Nov 2026 and has passed. This report's data runs to 30 Sep, so it can't show whether the decision was made. If it is still open, it should be decided now.

Supporting evidence from Q3:

  • Price was given as the reason in 6 of 17 lost deals (CRM).
  • The strategy is to win mid-market brokers by automating the exception, priced per load. Today's pricing doesn't match that strategy.
  • As a rough floor for per-load unit economics: inference spend divided by loads cleared with no touch comes to about $2.04 a load ($612K ÷ ~300K loads over 13 weeks). That figure is an upper bound on inference cost per cleared load, because inference also covers tasks that did not clear.

Churn was larger than expansion. The accounts that expanded grew 20% each, which shows the agent produces value once a customer adopts it. Most accounts never reached that point. There is no target date for $50M in the intake, but at Q3's net add of about $0.5M a quarter the gap is roughly $39M. Note: the finance close reports Q3 revenue of $10.3M against a $10.0M plan (+3%). Finance should reconcile the two before the board sees either number.

Strategy, for reference

Diagnosis: Dispatchers lose most of their day to exceptions that rules engines can't handle.
Policy: We win mid-market brokers by automating the exception, priced per load.
Actions: Raise agent task success to 90%. Get a first automated load inside 14 days.
Non-goals: Build a carrier marketplace. Ship on-premise.

Data sources: PostHog (analytics), Stripe (billing ARR, Jun and Sep snapshots), CRM (39 deals), Linear (tracker, commitments, customer requests), GitHub (91 deploys, 120 PRs, Apr–Sep), Incident tool (10 incidents, Apr–Sep), Eval harness (v13, v14), Finance close, HR system, Tool admin exports, Support desk (55 tickets), Developer survey.

Method notes: Q2 and Q3 are calendar quarters, and deploys per week use 13 weeks. Change failure rate is failed deploys divided by all deploys. Review time and time to restore are medians. ARR movements are calculated per account between the Jun and Sep billing snapshots.
