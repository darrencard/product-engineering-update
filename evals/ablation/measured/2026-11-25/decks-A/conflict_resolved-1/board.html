Decision requested

Approve a per-load pricing pilot with 10 customers. The alternative is to wait a quarter. Per-load pricing is the strategy's stated policy, and price was the reason for 6 of 17 lost deals in Q3.

Deadline: 4 Nov 2026. That date has passed. The source files don't record an outcome, so confirm status before this goes out.

What went well

  • The agent cleared 23,100 loads a week with no manual touch, up from 18,400.
  • Agent task success rose from 82% to 86% and cost per task fell from $0.41 to $0.33.
  • All 22 won deals cited the agent clearing exceptions. That supports the strategy.
  • 6 of 7 product commitments shipped, and weekly active dispatchers passed target (6,120 vs 6,000).
  • R&D spend was $4.20M, 1% under plan.

What needs attention

  • Churn. Four accounts left, taking $1.11M of ARR. That exceeded both new business (+$0.90M) and expansion (+$0.74M). Quarterly net revenue retention was 96%. We recommend a churn review of these four accounts in Q4.
  • Activation. It is flat at 41% against a 50% target. Self-serve carrier onboarding slipped, and onboarding was the reason for 18 of 55 support tickets.
  • Inference cost. Spend was $612K, 27.5% over plan, because volume grew faster than unit cost fell. Most of it goes to one model provider. A routing layer across 2 providers is due Dec 2026.
  • Integration. Missing TMS integration lost 11 of 17 deals, and integration errors drove 30 of 55 support tickets. No current bet addresses it. We propose scoping it in Q4 planning.
  • Release quality. Change failure rate rose to 7.7% against a 5% ceiling, even as deploys sped up. The VP Engineering owns the fix in Q4.

Data caveats

ARR is taken from billing ($10.91M). The finance close reports $10.1M, and the two still need reconciling. Loads cleared is taken from PostHog (23.1K). The warehouse shows 21.4K, which would fall below target. Both reconciliations are owned by the VP Engineering, with Finance on ARR, and due in Q4.
