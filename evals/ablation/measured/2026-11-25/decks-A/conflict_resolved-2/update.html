Q3 2026: the agent is clearing more loads, but activation and integrations are holding growth back

Quarter 1 Jul – 30 Sep 2026 · data as of 2026-09-30 · compared with Q2 2026 unless stated

Summary

  • North star met. Loads cleared with no manual touch averaged 23.1K/week against a 22K target, up 26% on Q2 (PostHog). The warehouse reports 21.4K, which would be a miss. We have not reconciled the two yet.
  • ARR is just under plan. Billing shows $10.91M against an $11.0M plan. New and expansion ARR ($1.64M) was offset by $1.11M from 4 churned accounts. Net revenue retention was 96% for the quarter. The finance close reports a different ARR ($10.10M).
  • Activation is the main miss: 41% against a 50% target (+2 pts). Guided activation shipped, but no lift shows yet. Self-serve carrier onboarding did not ship.
  • Integrations are a gap that no current bet covers. 11 of 17 lost deals cited "No TMS integration", and 30 of 55 support tickets were integration errors.
  • Delivery got faster but less stable. Deploys rose to 4.0/week and PR lead time fell to 38h. Change failure rate rose to 7.7% (target under 5%) and review time to 13h (target under 10h).
  • Inference spend was 27% over plan ($612K against $480K), even though agent cost per task fell 20%.
  • One decision is needed: the per-load pricing pilot (see below).

Decision needed: Approve a per-load pricing pilot

Options
Pilot with 10 customers, or wait a quarter
Recommendation
Run the pilot with 10 customers.
Why now
Our strategy is to win mid-market brokers by automating the exception, priced per load. Price was the reason in 6 of 17 lost deals this quarter. A pilot tests the pricing model before we roll it out to everyone.
Deadline
4 Nov 2026

The deadline has passed. It was 4 Nov 2026, and this update was compiled on 24 Nov 2026. If the pilot has already been decided, treat this as a record of that decision. If not, we need an answer now.

Bets and commitments

Multi-stop reroutes · Promise · Agent team

Expected: Task success 86% to 90%. Stop rule: Evals gain under 2 points by week 3.

Result: Shipped. On eval v14 (2026-09-22), task success is 86% (430/500), up from 82% on v13 (2026-06-20). That is short of 90%.

▲ Check the stop rule The bet's stated starting point (86%) matches the v14 result. One of two things is true: the 86% baseline was measured after the reroutes landed, or the reroutes have not moved the eval yet. If it is the second, the stop rule has triggered. The data doesn't show the ship date, so the Agent team needs to confirm which.

Demand signal: 4 customer requests from 3 customers (Bluepeak, Haulwell, Northwind).

Guided activation · Experiment · Activation team

Expected: Activation 41% to 50%. Stop rule: No lift in a 2 week live test.

Result: Shipped. Activation for Q3 is 41%, which equals the bet's baseline (Q2 was 39%). The lift has not shown up yet.

▲ Result pending Q3 is a quarterly average, so a feature that shipped late in the quarter would barely move it. Next step: read the 2-week live test against the stop rule and decide whether to continue or stop.

Data notes and open gaps

  • North star: PostHog (the source of truth) shows 23,100. The warehouse shows 21,400 (select avg(loads) from ns_weekly), 7.4% lower. That's the difference between beating the target and missing it. The warehouse query doesn't show a "no manual touch" filter, so the two may be measuring different things.
  • ARR: billing (the source of truth) sums to $10.911M for September. The finance close reports $10.100M, a $811K gap. This update uses billing.
  • Revenue: the finance close reports Q3 revenue of $10.3M (plan $10.0M). That is almost equal to full-year ARR, so it is probably a run-rate or annualized figure. I've left it out of the analysis until finance confirms what it represents.
  • Lead time: GitHub only gives us PR open-to-merge time. That leaves out commit-to-PR and merge-to-deploy, so it understates true lead time for change.
  • Investment mix: every tracker item is estimated at 1 point, so the mix reflects ticket counts, not effort.
  • Commitments: the tracker has no project names, so shipped status is self-reported in the intake. Support tickets have no dates.
  • Bet baselines: both bets' starting values equal the Q3 results. Bets need a dated baseline that's recorded before the work ships.
  • Owner for all items above: the VP Engineering, due Q4.
