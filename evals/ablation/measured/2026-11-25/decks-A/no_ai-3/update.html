Summary

  • North star beat target. Loads cleared with no manual touch averaged 23,100/week. The target was 22K and Q2 was 18,400, so that's up 26%. Weekly active dispatchers also beat target, at 6,120 against 6,000.
  • Activation is behind. It reached 41% against a 50% target, up only 2 points from Q2. Guided activation shipped. Self-serve carrier onboarding didn't.
  • Engineering shipped more, faster, but quality slipped. We deployed 4.0 times a week (3.0 in Q2) and lead time fell to 38h. The change failure rate rose to 7.7%, though, above the 5% target, and PR review time rose to 13h, above the 10h target.
  • Inference cost $612K, 27.5% over plan. Revenue beat plan by 3%. Total R&D spend was 1% under plan.
  • Integration problems are the loudest signal from the market. They caused 11 of 17 lost deals ("No TMS integration") and 30 of 55 support tickets. No current bet or strategy action addresses integration.
  • Two of our own success measures aren't tracked anywhere. We can't yet report agent task success (strategy action #1 and the Multi-stop reroutes bet) or cost per successful task. Closing those gaps is owned by the VP Engineering, due in Q4 (see the list).

Decision needed

Approve a per-load pricing pilot

Options: run a pilot with 10 customers, or wait a quarter.
Recommendation: run the pilot with 10 customers.
Deadline: 4 Nov 2026.

Why it matters: the strategy says we win mid-market brokers "priced per load", but we have no live per-load pricing yet. Price caused 6 of 17 lost deals in the CRM export (35%), second only to missing TMS integration.

Customer and market signal

What this tells us

  • The agent is why we win. Every won deal cites "Agent clears exceptions", which supports the strategy's diagnosis.
  • Integration is why we lose. Missing TMS integration explains 65% of losses, and integration errors are 55% of support tickets. Neither strategy action, nor either bet, addresses it. Question for leadership: should integration become an explicit Q4 bet?
  • Onboarding and churn. All 4 accounts that churned (A037–A040, $1.11M of June ARR) filed onboarding tickets in the quarter. Self-serve carrier onboarding, the commitment meant to help, didn't ship. With only four accounts, treat this as a lead to follow up, not proof of cause.

Where engineering time went

Keep-the-lights-on work took 22% of completed items against a 15% plan. That 7-point overrun came mostly out of new capabilities, growth, and improving the core. All of the KTLO work, and all of the Platform & scale work, sat with the Platform team, which completed 43 of the 100 items. Together with the rising change failure rate, this points to platform load as a constraint going into Q4.

By product line: Dispatch Agent (Expand stage) 31 items, Core Dispatch (Sustain) 26 items, Platform (Sustain) 43 items. Items were completed by month as follows: July 53, August 37, September 10. The tail-off in September is worth asking about.

Data gaps and what we'll fix

Owner: the VP Engineering. Due: Q4.

  1. Agent task success isn't tracked. It's strategy action #1 and the target of the Multi-stop reroutes bet (86% to 90%). Add it to PostHog, along with the eval results the stop rule depends on.
  2. Cost per successful task isn't tracked. Without it we can't evaluate "Cheaper agent retries" or explain the inference overrun.
  3. ARR sources disagree. The Stripe export ($10.9M ARR) can't be reconciled with Finance revenue ($10.3M for the quarter). Agree on one source so we can report against the $50M objective.
  4. Commitments aren't traceable in Linear. Export items have generic titles and no link to a commitment, so neither shipped status nor ship dates can be checked.
  5. Deploys aren't linked to PRs. Lead time currently stops at merge.
  6. Bet results are missing. No eval or live-test results were provided for either stop rule. The Guided activation baseline (41%) also differs from the Q2 figure (39%).
  7. Request hygiene. 2 of 8 customer requests (KES-388) have no Linear link, and 1 predates the quarter. Team names also differ between the intake and Linear (for example, "Agent team" and "Dispatch Agent team").

How these numbers were computed

  • Quarters are calendar quarters: Q2 is 1 Apr – 30 Jun and Q3 is 1 Jul – 30 Sep 2026. Weekly rates divide by 13 weeks.
  • Change failure rate is failed deploys divided by all deploys. Time to restore is the mean of restored minus started across incidents in the quarter. Review time is the mean of review_hours for PRs merged in the quarter. Rework rate is the share of those PRs flagged as reworked.
  • The ARR bridge compares each account's June and September values in Stripe. Accounts missing in September count as churn, and accounts missing in June count as new.
  • Investment mix counts completed Linear items by work_type.
