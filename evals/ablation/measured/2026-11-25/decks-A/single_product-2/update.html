2026-Q3: we hit the north star, but activation and onboarding are behind

Period: 1 Jul – 30 Sep 2026 · Data as of 30 Sep 2026 · From the CPTO · Audience: leadership team

Decision needed: approve a per-load pricing pilot

Recommendation: run the pilot with 10 customers. The other option is to wait a quarter. Deadline: 4 Nov 2026.

Why now: price was the reason for 6 of 17 lost deals this quarter. Per-load pricing is the policy in our strategy, which is to win mid-market brokers by automating the exception, priced per load. A pilot lets us test it before we change list pricing.

This report covers data through 30 Sep 2026 and was compiled on 24 Nov 2026, after the 4 Nov deadline. If the pilot decision has already been made, treat this section as a record.

Summary

  • North star hit. Loads cleared with no manual touch averaged 23,100 per week. The target was 22K and Q2 was 18,400, so that is up 26% quarter on quarter. Weekly active dispatchers (6,120) also beat target.
  • Activation missed. 41% of accounts got a first automated load within 14 days, against a 50% target (Q2: 39%). Self-serve carrier onboarding, the one commitment that didn't ship, was aimed at this metric. All 4 accounts that churned ($1.11M ARR) had opened onboarding tickets.
  • Integration is our biggest commercial gap. "No TMS integration" was the reason for 11 of 17 lost deals. Integration errors were also the top support theme, at 30 of 55 tickets. No strategy action or Q3 bet addresses it, and no customer request in the tracker is linked to it.
  • Agent quality and unit cost improved, but inference spend is over plan. The agent passed 86% of eval cases on v14, up from 82%, and cost per task fell from $0.41 to $0.33. Even so, inference spend was $612K against a $480K plan (+28%), because loads grew faster than unit cost fell. Most of that spend still goes to one model provider.
  • We ship faster but less safely. Deploys rose from 3.0 to 4.0 per week and lead time fell from 52h to 38h. But the change failure rate is 7.7% (target under 5%) and PR review time is 13h (target under 10h). Both got worse this quarter.
  • Keep-the-lights-on work took 22% of capacity against a 15% plan. Most of that came out of new capabilities and growth.

Strategy, as a reminder

Diagnosis: Dispatchers lose most of their day to exceptions that rules engines can't handle.

Policy: We win mid-market brokers by automating the exception, priced per load.

Actions: Raise agent task success to 90%. Get each new account to a first automated load within 14 days.

Non-goals: Build a carrier marketplace. Ship on-premise.

Company objective: grow ARR to $50M (owner: CEO). Product line: Dispatch Agent, stage Expand.

Why we lost deals in Q3

17 lost deals, from the CRM

Support tickets by theme

55 tickets, from the support desk

  • Integration: it causes the most lost deals and the most support tickets. Thirty accounts (A001–A030) each filed an integration-error ticket. Nothing on the roadmap targets it today. I'd like leadership to decide whether integration belongs in the Q4 plan.
  • Onboarding and churn: each of the 4 churned accounts (A037–A040) had an onboarding ticket. Combined with the activation miss and the slip on self-serve carrier onboarding, this makes onboarding our clearest retention lever.
  • Why we win: all 22 won deals gave the same reason, "Agent clears exceptions." That matches the strategy's diagnosis.
  • Customer requests: Multi-stop reroutes had the most linked requests (4 from Northwind, Haulwell and Bluepeak), and it shipped. Bulk load import (KES-412) has 2 requests from Haulwell and Tidewater. Carrier rating export (KES-388) has 2 requests from Bluepeak and Tidewater, but neither is linked to the issue.

Data gaps & caveats

Owner: the VP Engineering. Due: Q4.

  • ARR vs. revenue. Billing ARR ($10.9M) and the quarter's finance revenue ($10.3M) don't reconcile. Until they do, we can't report progress toward the $50M objective.
  • Lead time. PRs aren't linked to deploys, so we can only measure lead time from PR opened to merged.
  • Tracker. Item titles are generic and every estimate is 1. The investment mix is therefore an item count, and we can't trace commitments to tracker items.
  • Bet readouts. There are only two eval runs and no activation live-test result, so we can't apply either bet's stop rule.
  • Customer requests. Two requests (R-007 and R-999) aren't linked to an issue, and R-999 is dated before Q3. TMS integration has no linked requests, even though it is the top reason we lose deals.
  • Cost per successful task. The eval harness reports cost per task. It's not clear whether that means per successful task, which is what the commitment named.
